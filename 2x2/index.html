// The 4 + 1 rule

Five movements. Every major muscle.

Each workout has four upper-body movements—two pushes, two pulls—plus one leg exercise. No arm day, no endless isolation work. The balance of pushing and pulling protects shoulders that spend all day hunched over a desk.

1 · PushChest pressChest, front delts, triceps
2 · PullRowMid-back, rear delts, biceps
3 · PullPull-upLats, biceps
4 · PushOverhead pressShoulders, triceps, core
+1 · LegsSquat / RDLAlternating: squat in A, Romanian deadlift in B

// Sample: Workout A

Legs first, then two supersets.

Upper-body movements are paired push + pull, so one muscle group rests while the other works. That's how the whole session fits into about 40 minutes.

Workout B uses the same 4 + 1 with different variations: Romanian deadlift, incline press, chest-supported row, standing press, chin-up.

Rule 1

Hard sets only

Set 1 ends with 1–2 reps in reserve. Set 2 goes to technical failure (0–1 in reserve). With only two sets, every set has to count.

Rule 2

Double progression

Add reps until you hit the top of the range on both sets. Then add the smallest weight jump and start again from the bottom.

// FAQ

Only two sets? Everyone at the gym does four.

Two sets—but truly hard ones, twice a week. The first sets deliver most of the results; every additional set delivers less. For a busy person, 2×2 is a sensible minimum that works for years.

Won't my arms be weak without curls?

Your biceps work hard in rows and pull-ups, your triceps in both presses. You can add one optional arm exercise—never at the expense of the 4 + 1.

Can I do it at home?

Yes. Adjustable dumbbells, a bench, and a pull-up bar cover the whole plan. The book also includes a no-equipment travel version.

I'm over 40. Is this for me?

Low volume means less stress on joints and tendons and more time to recover. The book has a full chapter on training over 40 and working around sore shoulders, knees, and lower backs.

Can't do a single pull-up?

Very common. Start with lat pulldowns, band-assisted pull-ups, or slow negatives. The book walks you through all three.
